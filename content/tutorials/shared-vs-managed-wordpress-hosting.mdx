## The core difference

**Shared WordPress hosting** puts your site on a server with dozens of others. It's $2–6/mo, you manage updates and backups yourself, and performance depends partly on your neighbors.

**Managed WordPress hosting** gives you dedicated resources (often VPS-grade) plus the provider handling updates, backups, security, staging, and caching. It's $20–35/mo — you pay to offload the work.

The question isn't which is "better" — it's which phase your site is in.

## Shared vs managed, side by side

| | Shared | Managed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Price | $2–6/mo | $20–35/mo |
| Performance | Good (LiteSpeed) to poor (Apache) | Excellent, consistent |
| Updates | You | Provider (auto) |
| Backups | Usually included | Included, with restores |
| Staging | Rarely | Yes |
| Security | Basic | WAF, scanning, hardening |
| Support | General | WordPress experts |
| Best for | Blogs, small business sites | Revenue-critical sites, agencies |

## How much does the performance actually differ?

From our six-month benchmark program:

- **Hostinger shared** — 142ms TTFB, 99.99% uptime. This is the surprise: modern LiteSpeed + NVMe shared hosting is *fast*.
- **SiteGround shared** — 185ms TTFB, 9.4/10 support.
- **Cloudways managed VPS** — 124ms TTFB.
- **Kinsta managed** — 98ms TTFB, 100% uptime.

The gap between good shared and managed is real but small — **roughly 40–80ms**. The bigger differences are reliability under traffic spikes and the maintenance work you don't have to do.

## When to stay on shared hosting

- Your site is a **blog, portfolio, or small business site**
- Traffic is under ~30–50k visits/month
- You don't mind (or enjoy) handling updates and backups
- You're cost-sensitive — $3/mo beats $30/mo for the same outcome

The [cheap WordPress hosting](/tutorials/cheap-wordpress-hosting) guide covers the best shared plans in detail.

## When to upgrade to managed

- Your site **drives revenue** — one outage costs more than a year of hosting
- You'd rather **not touch updates/security** at all
- You run **WooCommerce** with real traffic
- You're an **agency** with client sites and SLAs
- You need **staging** to test changes safely

## Frequently asked questions

**Is managed WordPress hosting worth the cost?**
If your site generates revenue, yes — uptime, speed, and support pay for themselves. If it's a hobby blog, premium shared hosting is a better use of $30/mo.

**Can I switch from shared to managed WordPress hosting later?**
Yes — most managed hosts include free migration, so the upgrade is low-risk. Our [move WordPress to a new host](/tutorials/move-wordpress-to-new-host) guide walks through it.

**Is shared WordPress hosting fast enough?**
Modern shared hosting (LiteSpeed + NVMe) sustains 142–185ms TTFB in our tests — fast enough for most sites. Older Apache-based shared plans are the slow ones to avoid.

**What is the best managed WordPress hosting?**
Kinsta for performance, WP Engine for enterprise stability, Cloudways for value. The [managed WordPress hosting](/tutorials/managed-wordpress-hosting) guide has the full comparison.
