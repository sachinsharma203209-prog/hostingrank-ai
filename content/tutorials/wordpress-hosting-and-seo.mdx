## Does hosting matter for SEO?

Yes — directly. Three of the four Core Web Vitals that Google uses as ranking signals are **infrastructure dependent**:

- **LCP** (loading) — a slow TTFB delays everything that follows
- **INP** (interactivity) — server response time under load
- **CLS** (layout) — mostly front-end, but slow rendering worsens it

On top of that: **uptime** (Google penalizes sites it can't crawl reliably), **HTTPS** (a hard requirement since 2014), and **location** (server distance to your audience). Your host doesn't just host your site — it sets a performance ceiling your SEO can never exceed.

## The numbers that matter

| Metric | Good | Poor |
| --- | --- | --- |
| TTFB | Under 200ms | Over 500ms |
| Uptime | 99.9%+ | Below 99.9% |
| SSL | Force-HTTPS | HTTP mixed content |
| Server location | Near your audience | Far from your audience |

The best hosts in our [benchmarks](/benchmarks) hit **sub-200ms TTFB** (Hostinger 142ms, Cloudways 124ms, Kinsta 98ms). The worst shared hosts run 400–700ms — and Google treats that as slow regardless of how well you optimize your content.

## How to check your site's speed signals

1. **PageSpeed Insights** (pagespeed.web.dev) — your Core Web Vitals, especially LCP
2. **WebPageTest** — waterfall view showing exactly where the delay is
3. **Chrome DevTools Network tab** — check the server's TTFB for a document request

If **TTFB is the red bar**, your hosting is the problem — not images, not plugins. That's the single most common finding on slow sites. Our [speed guide](/tutorials/speed-up-wordpress) shows how to isolate it.

## The hosting decisions that help SEO

### 1. Pick a fast stack
LiteSpeed + NVMe beats Apache + HDD every time. This is the difference between the [best WordPress hosting](/best/wordpress-hosting) plans and the bargain bins. 142ms vs 680ms TTFB isn't a minor difference — it's a different class of site.

### 2. Serve close to your audience
Choose a data center in (or near) your audience's country. An India site on a Mumbai server beats one on a US server by 100–150ms of latency. Our [best hosting by country](/best/hosting-india) guides cover this.

### 3. Add HTTPS and force it
Free SSL is standard everywhere now — set it up (see [email & SSL setup](/tutorials/setup-email-ssl)) and make sure your WordPress **Site Address** uses `https://`. Mixed-content warnings are both a user-experience and ranking problem.

### 4. Add a CDN for global visitors
A CDN (see [WordPress CDN](/tutorials/wordpress-hosting-cdn)) serves static assets from the edge, cutting LCP for visitors far from your server. It won't fix a slow origin, but it's the right second layer.

### 5. Maintain uptime and backups
Google crawls when it crawls. Consistent 99.9%+ uptime (see [uptime guide](/tutorials/wordpress-hosting-uptime)) plus regular [backups](/tutorials/wordpress-backups) means no 404-walls and no losing indexed pages.

## What doesn't matter for SEO

- **Premium hosting ≠ better rankings automatically** — a fast budget host outranks a slow managed host. Only pay for managed ([managed hosting](/tutorials/managed-wordpress-hosting)) if you value the convenience too.
- **Your host's "SEO tools"** — built-in SEO feature blobs are marketing. Real SEO is speed, content, and technical correctness, not a host dashboard tab.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does hosting speed affect Google rankings?**
Yes — TTFB and Core Web Vitals are ranking factors. Sub-200ms TTFB is the standard the top hosts in our benchmarks deliver; slower hosts cap your ceiling.

**Is cheap hosting bad for SEO?**
Not inherently — Hostinger at $2.99/mo posts 142ms TTFB. Cheap hosting is only bad for SEO when it's slow or flaky. See [cheap WordPress hosting](/tutorials/cheap-wordpress-hosting).

**Does the location of the server matter for SEO?**
For crawl and delivery speed to your audience, yes. Choose a data center near your users, or use a CDN to compensate.

**Can I improve SEO by switching hosts?**
Often dramatically — a 500ms TTFB drop plus stable uptime is a genuine Core Web Vitals improvement. Use our [migration guide](/tutorials/wordpress-hosting-migration) to switch without losing rankings.

**What is a good TTFB for SEO?**
Under 200ms is excellent, under 400ms acceptable. Above 600ms you should move or fix your stack. All our [provider reviews](/reviews) publish measured TTFB.
