## Why you shouldn't send newsletters from hosting

Your [WordPress hosting](/tutorials/what-is-wordpress-hosting) is for your website — not for sending thousands of emails. Servers that blast newsletters get flagged by spam engines within days, and shared hosting ISPs block bulk sending outright. The rules:

- **Transactional mail** (password resets, order notifications) → SMTP service. [SMTP guide](/tutorials/wordpress-email-smtp)
- **Bulk mail** (newsletters) → an email marketing platform

For your business mailbox, see the [domain email setup](/tutorials/wordpress-email-setup). Newsletters are a separate lane.

## The best newsletter services for WordPress

| Service | Free tier | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| MailerLite | 1,000 subs, 12k emails/mo | Best free tier, simple forms |
| Brevo (Sendinblue) | 300 emails/day | High volume, good pricing |
| Mailchimp | 500 contacts, 1k sends/mo | The familiar name, app ecosystem |
| MailPoet | 500 subs (WordPress-native) | Everything inside WordPress |

For WordPress sites, **MailerLite** and **Brevo** offer the best free plans; **MailPoet** is the one that runs entirely inside your dashboard (it still sends via a delivery service, so deliverability stays good).

## How to connect a newsletter service

### 1. Create the service account
Sign up at the provider, create your list, and confirm your **sending domain** (they'll give you SPF/DKIM records to publish — see [deliverability](/tutorials/wordpress-email-deliverability)).

### 2. Install the integration
- **Mailchimp:** the official Mailchimp plugin, or **MC4WP: Mailchimp for WordPress** (free, better forms)
- **MailerLite:** the official MailerLite plugin
- **Brevo:** the official Brevo (Sendinblue) plugin
- **MailPoet:** the plugin *is* the newsletter service — add your signup form as a block

### 3. Add opt-in forms
Place forms where readers actually subscribe:
- After content (the most effective spot)
- Sidebar and footer widgets
- A dedicated landing page
- Popup on exit intent (some services include this free)

### 4. Test the double opt-in
Enable **double opt-in** — a confirmation email protects your list quality and your sender reputation. Confirm the confirmation email lands in the inbox (not spam) before going live.

## Deliverability for newsletters

Newsletter success is a deliverability game:

- **Warm up** — start with small sends to engaged subscribers, then scale. A cold new domain blasting 50k emails is a fast path to blocklists
- **Clean your list** — remove bounces and non-openers; engagement is the #1 spam filter signal
- **Watch spam rate** — above 0.1% triggers flags. Providers report this in your dashboard
- **Publish SPF/DKIM/DMARC** — the [deliverability guide](/tutorials/wordpress-email-deliverability) has the full setup

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I send newsletters from my WordPress hosting?**
Technically some shared hosts allow it, but don't — bulk sends from hosting servers get flagged as spam and hurt your domain reputation. Use a newsletter service.

**What is the best free newsletter service for WordPress?**
MailerLite (1,000 subs free) and Brevo (300 emails/day free) lead. MailPoet if you want everything inside WordPress. Full guide above.

**Mailchimp or Brevo for WordPress?**
Mailchimp for its ecosystem and plugin support; Brevo for cheaper high-volume sending. Both integrate cleanly with WordPress plugins.

**How do I add a newsletter signup form to WordPress?**
Install the service's plugin (or MC4WP for Mailchimp) and add the form block to posts, widgets, or a landing page. The setup is in this guide.

**Why is my newsletter going to spam?**
Usually missing SPF/DKIM/DMARC, a new domain sending too much too fast, or a dirty list. Fix the records ([guide](/tutorials/wordpress-email-deliverability)), warm up, and clean your list.
