## WordPress hosting for beginners, in one paragraph

WordPress hosting is a service that stores your website's files and database and serves them to visitors. As a beginner you don't need to understand servers, PHP, or MySQL — you need a host with **one-click WordPress installation**, a **free SSL certificate**, and support that answers quickly when you get stuck. The rest is point-and-click.

The best beginner-friendly host right now is **Hostinger** (see the [Hostinger review](/reviews/hostinger-review)) — a 142ms TTFB, a one-click installer, and 24/7 chat support that answered in under two minutes in our tests. Bluehost is the alternative if you want WordPress.org's official stamp and more guided onboarding.

## What to look for as a beginner

- **One-click WordPress install** — no command line, no databases
- **Free SSL** — the padlock in the browser, required for trust and SEO
- **Free site migration** — if you ever move from another host
- **Daily backups** — recover your site if something breaks
- **24/7 support** — chat that actually responds
- **A simple control panel** — hPanel or cPanel, not raw SSH

You do **not** need: the cheapest possible plan, a VPS, cloud hosting, or anything "managed enterprise." Start simple.

## How much should a beginner pay?

- **Free** — fine for learning, limits everything. See [free WordPress hosting](/tutorials/free-wordpress-hosting).
- **$2–5/mo** — the sweet spot: Hostinger Single/Premium, SiteGround, Bluehost.
- **$10+/mo** — unnecessary for your first site. Upgrade later if you outgrow it.

## Getting your first WordPress site live

Our [step-by-step Hostinger install guide](/tutorials/install-wordpress-on-hostinger) walks through it, but here's the whole journey in six steps:

1. **Buy a plan** — Hostinger Premium is the best starting point (~$3/mo, free domain year one).
2. **Choose your domain** — pick a name, get it free or for ~$10/yr.
3. **Run the one-click installer** — hPanel provisions WordPress in about a minute.
4. **Log in to wp-admin** — your site's dashboard, where you write posts and add plugins.
5. **Pick a theme** — use the default or browse the free theme directory.
6. **Set up SSL** — usually automatic; the padlock should appear.

That's genuinely it. A beginner can go from zero to live site in 15 minutes.

## Common beginner mistakes to avoid

- **Buying the cheapest plan for 4 years** — try a 1-year term first
- **Ignoring the renewal price** — intro prices double or triple at renewal
- **Installing 30 plugins** — each one adds load; start minimal
- **Skipping backups** — enable the host's daily backups in the panel
- **Paying for "unlimited"** — entry plans still cap CPU/RAM

## Should beginners buy managed WordPress hosting?

Generally no — not yet. Managed hosting (Kinsta, WP Engine at $20–35/mo) removes update work you might not even mind doing yet. Start on a good shared plan, and revisit [managed WordPress hosting](/tutorials/managed-wordpress-hosting) once your site has real traffic or revenue.

**What is the best WordPress hosting for beginners?**

**Is WordPress hosting hard to set up?**
No. Modern hosts install WordPress for you in under a minute. You spend your first day picking a theme and writing your first post, not configuring servers.

**Do I need a domain name for WordPress hosting?**
Yes — visitors reach you through a domain. Most mid-tier plans include a free domain for year one.

**Can I start with free WordPress hosting as a beginner?**
You can learn on it, but free plans come with ads, subdomain URLs, and no backups. For your first real site, $3/mo removes all the friction.

**What's the difference between WordPress.com and WordPress hosting?**
WordPress.com is a closed platform you rent. WordPress hosting (this guide) runs the self-hosted WordPress.org software, giving you full control over themes, plugins, and your data.
