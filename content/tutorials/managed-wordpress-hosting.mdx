## What is managed WordPress hosting?

Managed WordPress hosting is a hosting service where the provider takes responsibility for the WordPress-specific work you'd otherwise do yourself. That means:

- **Automatic WordPress core, theme, and plugin updates**
- **Daily automated backups** with one-click restore
- **Staging environments** so you can test before going live
- **Security hardening** — WAF, malware scanning, and DDoS protection
- **Performance tuning** — server-side caching, CDN, and PHP optimization
- **Expert WordPress support** available 24/7

You pay more than shared hosting, but you trade your time and risk for predictable uptime, speed, and safety. This is the WordPress hosting meaning that sites with revenue care about.

## Who should use managed WordPress hosting?

- **Businesses and e-commerce stores** where downtime costs money
- **Agencies** managing many client sites with SLAs
- **Non-technical owners** who want a hands-off experience
- **High-traffic blogs** that need guaranteed performance under spikes

Who shouldn't? Hobby blogs and test sites — a good shared plan like Hostinger is more cost-effective. Our [managed hosting rankings](/best/managed-hosting) include full data.

## The best managed WordPress hosting, tested

### Kinsta — the performance king
Kinsta posted the fastest numbers we've measured: **98ms global TTFB**, 100% uptime across our entire six-month test, and sub-2-minute median support responses. Infrastructure is Google Cloud, and every plan includes Cloudflare edge caching at no extra cost. Read the [Kinsta review](/reviews/kinsta-review).

### WP Engine — the enterprise standard
WP Engine is the mature agency platform: enterprise SLAs, a plugin approval process that keeps sites stable, and a 60-day money-back guarantee. Its 170ms TTFB trails Kinsta slightly but its developer tooling is best-in-class. Read the [WP Engine review](/reviews/wp-engine-review).

### Cloudways — the developer favorite
Cloudways wraps managed convenience around your choice of AWS, Google Cloud, DigitalOcean, or Vultr. You get staging, Redis, and a clean dashboard — and the 124ms TTFB to show for it. It's pay-as-you-go with no lock-in. Read the [Cloudways review](/reviews/cloudways-review).

### SiteGround — best value managed experience
SiteGround gives you many managed features — automatic updates, daily backups, a performance plugin, and excellent support — at shared-hosting prices starting near $4/mo. It's our recommendation for most single-site beginners. Read the [SiteGround review](/reviews/siteground-review).

## Managed vs shared WordPress hosting

The tradeoff is cost against control and convenience. Shared hosting is $2–6/mo and you handle updates, backups, and speed yourself (we have a [speed-up guide](/tutorials/speed-up-wordpress) for that). Managed hosting is $20–35/mo and the provider handles all of it.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is managed WordPress hosting worth the extra cost?**
If your site drives revenue, yes — the uptime, speed, and support pay for themselves after a single incident. For a hobby blog, premium shared hosting is a better use of money.

**What is the best managed WordPress hosting?**
Kinsta is our overall pick for performance and support. WP Engine is the strongest enterprise alternative, SiteGround the best value, and Cloudways the best choice if you want your own cloud infrastructure.

**Does managed WordPress hosting include a free migration?**
Most do. Kinsta, WP Engine, SiteGround, and Cloudways all include free migration from your current host. Our [migration guide](/tutorials/migrate-wordpress-to-cloudways) walks through the process on Cloudways.

**Can I install any plugin on managed WordPress hosting?**
Kinsta and WP Engine maintain approved-plugin lists and may block resource-heavy plugins to protect the server. Cloudways and SiteGround are more permissive.
