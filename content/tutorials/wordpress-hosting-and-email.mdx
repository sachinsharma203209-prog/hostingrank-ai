## Does your WordPress host include email?

Most WordPress hosting plans include email accounts: Hostinger, SiteGround, Bluehost, and A2 Hosting all bundle mailboxes with their shared plans. The hosting and email usually come together — but "included" isn't the same as "good."

The honest version: **shared hosting email works for light use**. If email is mission-critical (a business sending and receiving hundreds of messages a day, or doing cold outreach), your host's mail will eventually cause deliverability problems.

## Included email vs dedicated email services

| | Host email (shared) | Google Workspace / Microsoft 365 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cost | Free with plan | $6–12/mo per mailbox |
| Reliability | Good enough | Excellent |
| Deliverability | Variable | Best-in-class |
| Spam filtering | Basic | Strong |
| Downtime | Tied to server | Rare |
| Storage | 5–20GB | 30GB+ |

The rule of thumb: **receive with included email, send with a dedicated service.** Your custom domain inbox (you@yourdomain.com) is the professional must-have; the question is just where it lives.

## Setting up email on your WordPress host

**With most shared hosts:**

1. Create the mailbox in your control panel (hPanel/cPanel — usually under "Email")
2. Set your domain's MX records to the host's mail server (the panel often does this for you)
3. Add the account to Gmail/Outlook/Apple Mail
4. Optionally configure SPF, DKIM, and DMARC so your mail doesn't land in spam

The MX record step is where most setup goes wrong. Your host's setup wizard usually handles it automatically — see [how to set up a custom domain email](/tutorials/wordpress-email-setup) for the full walkthrough.

## The modern approach: separate email from hosting

More and more, WordPress users keep hosting and email separate:

- **Google Workspace** ($6/user/mo) — the standard for small business email
- **Microsoft 365** ($6/user/mo) — better for heavy Outlook users
- **Zoho Mail** — free tier up to 5 mailboxes, then $1/user/mo
- **Hostinger email** — solid bundled option, easier migration between Hostinger plans

The advantage: your email never goes down with your website, and deliverability improves. This matters most if you send a lot of mail — newsletters, order confirmations, client correspondence.

## Spam and deliverability basics

If your mail lands in spam, check these in order:

1. **SPF** — declares which servers may send mail for your domain
2. **DKIM** — cryptographically signs outgoing mail
3. **DMARC** — tells receiving servers what to do with forged mail
4. **Warm-up** — a brand-new domain/address sending volume needs a ramp-up period

Most hosts set SPF/DKIM automatically. Our [email deliverability tutorial](/tutorials/wordpress-email-deliverability) walks through verification and fixes.

## Transactional email: separate from inboxes

If WordPress sends emails to your users (password resets, WooCommerce order notifications, form submissions), that's **transactional email** — a different thing from mailboxes. Server PHP mail is unreliable; use a service instead:

- **Mailgun / SendGrid / Postmark** — free tiers for modest volume
- **SMTP plugin** (e.g., WP Mail SMTP) to route WordPress through the service

This keeps your important site mail out of spam and off your server. More in [WordPress email delivery setup](/tutorials/wordpress-email-smtp).

## Frequently asked questions

**Does WordPress hosting include email?**
Most shared hosts do — Hostinger, SiteGround, Bluehost, and A2 bundle mailboxes with their plans. Managed and VPS tiers (Cloudways, Kinsta, WP Engine) don't include mailboxes.

**Can I use my domain for email without hosting?**
Yes — buy the domain anywhere and point MX records at a dedicated email service like Google Workspace or Zoho. No hosting required.

**Should I get email from my web host or Google Workspace?**
For light use, host email is fine and free. For professional reliability and deliverability, Google Workspace at $6/mo per mailbox is worth it.

**What is the best free email for a custom domain?**
Zoho Mail — free for up to 5 mailboxes with your own domain. See [free WordPress hosting](/tutorials/free-wordpress-hosting) for the full budget stack.

**Does email affect WordPress hosting performance?**
Shared hosting email shares the server with your site. High-volume sending can degrade site speed — another reason to offload heavy email to a dedicated service.
