## GoDaddy WordPress hosting, honestly assessed

GoDaddy is one of the biggest names in domains and hosting, and its **WordPress hosting** line is genuinely different from its legacy shared plans. The managed WordPress tiers run on a tuned stack with automatic updates, caching, and free SSL. But "different" isn't the same as "best" — and in our benchmarks, GoDaddy's performance and pricing trail the value leaders.

The short takeaway: GoDaddy WordPress hosting is fine if you already have your domain there and want one dashboard for everything. If you're buying hosting on merit, hosts like **Hostinger** or **SiteGround** deliver better speed and lower renewal prices. See our [best WordPress hosting](/best/wordpress-hosting) page for the current winners.

## GoDaddy WordPress hosting plans

GoDaddy sells both **shared WordPress hosting** and **managed WordPress hosting (WooCommerce-ready)** tiers:

- **Basic WordPress** — one site, ~10GB storage, limited visitors
- **Deluxe WordPress** — more storage and site count
- **Ultimate WordPress** — unmetered bandwidth, more resources
- **Ecommerce WordPress** — WooCommerce optimized with more CPU/RAM

The "deluxe" and higher tiers include automatic WordPress core updates, daily backups (on some plans), and a free SSL. Renewal pricing — which can be **2–3x the intro price** — is the number to focus on, not the first-year teaser.

## How GoDaddy performs

In independent tests across the market, GoDaddy's shared WordPress plans typically produce **TTFB of 400–700ms** and uptime around 99.9%. That's functional but noticeably slower than LiteSpeed + NVMe competitors. Its managed tiers are better, but at those prices you're in [Kinsta](/reviews/kinsta-review) or [WP Engine](/reviews/wp-engine-review) territory — where the performance is best-in-class.

If you're comparing budget options, our [Hostinger vs Bluehost](/comparisons/hostinger-vs-bluehost) data gives a sense of how much stack choice moves the needle: NVMe + LiteSpeed vs Apache is a 2–4x TTFB difference.

## What GoDaddy WordPress hosting does well

- **Domains + hosting in one place** — the biggest practical reason people choose it
- **Managed WordPress features** on higher tiers: auto-updates, caching, staging (on ecommerce)
- **24/7 phone support** — rarer in the industry than you'd think
- **Free SSL and CDN basics** included

## Where it falls short

- **Performance** lags modern NVMe/LiteSpeed hosts
- **Renewal pricing** jumps aggressively after year one
- **Entry plans** are heavily resource-limited
- **Upsells** at checkout (site builder, SEO, email)

## Frequently asked questions

**Is GoDaddy good for WordPress hosting?**
It's adequate, not great. Managed tiers are stable and convenient if your domain lives at GoDaddy, but performance and renewal pricing trail the value leaders. For most sites, Hostinger or SiteGround is the better buy.

**What is the best GoDaddy WordPress hosting plan?**
The Ecommerce plan is the only one worth considering for a real store; the cheaper tiers are too resource-limited. Even then, a comparable VPS or managed plan from a specialist is usually better value.

**Is GoDaddy WordPress hosting good for small business?**
For a simple brochure site, it works. For a site you expect to grow or rank well, the slower TTFB and higher renewals make alternatives more attractive.
