## The two ways to open an online store

Shopify and WordPress + WooCommerce are the two dominant routes to ecommerce. They're fundamentally different bets:

- **Shopify** — a hosted platform: you rent the store, they own the infrastructure
- **WordPress + WooCommerce** — self-hosted: you own everything, you pick the hosting

This is the ecommerce version of the [WordPress vs site builders](/tutorials/wordpress-hosting-vs-site-builders) question — except the stakes (your revenue) are higher.

## Cost: the long game

| | WordPress + WooCommerce | Shopify |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Setup | Hosting ~$5/mo + free WooCommerce | $39/mo (Basic) or $29/mo annual |
| Transaction fees | 0% (your gateway decides) | 2.9% + 30¢ (Shopify Payments), +0.5–2% otherwise |
| Themes/plugins | Free + paid options | $20–40/mo apps, $100s for themes |
| Your data | Fully yours, portable | Inside Shopify |

The hidden Shopify math: **transaction fees**. On $10k/month of sales with Shopify Payments, that's ~$320/mo in fees alone — versus $5/mo hosting on WooCommerce. Over a year, a serious store pays for itself in the comparison.

## Control and lock-in

**WordPress + WooCommerce:**
- Every plugin, theme, and checkout tweak is possible
- You own the database and can [migrate hosts](/tutorials/wordpress-hosting-migration) freely
- Extend with payment gateways, subscriptions, multi-vendor, anything

**Shopify:**
- Fast, reliable, beautifully maintained — you never touch servers
- You're limited to the app store and theme constraints
- Your store lives on Shopify; leaving means rebuilding on a platform that exports only basic data

## Performance and SEO

- **WooCommerce speed is your responsibility** — with good [WooCommerce hosting](/tutorials/woocommerce-hosting) (sub-200ms TTFB) you can beat Shopify on load time.
- **Shopify** — reliable by default, but you can't tune the server, and TTFB is typically 300–500ms.
- **SEO** — WordPress gives full technical control (schema, redirects, speed). Shopify is fine for basics but restricts advanced optimization.

For content-heavy or SEO-driven stores, WordPress is the stronger long-term play.

## The verdict

**Choose WordPress + WooCommerce if:**
- You want no transaction fees and full control
- You're planning to grow and scale
- SEO and content marketing are part of the strategy
- You're comfortable with ~an hour a month of [maintenance](/tutorials/wordpress-hosting-maintenance)

**Choose Shopify if:**
- You want a store live this weekend with zero server thinking
- You're happy to pay fees for convenience
- Your catalog is simple and you don't need heavy customization

For most serious sellers, **WordPress + WooCommerce on good hosting is cheaper and more flexible** — the transaction fees alone usually decide it.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is WooCommerce cheaper than Shopify?**
Yes, dramatically at volume — $5/mo hosting + 0% transaction fees versus $39/mo + 2.9% + 30¢ per transaction. For a $10k/mo store, WooCommerce saves roughly $300+/mo.

**Can I migrate from Shopify to WooCommerce?**
Yes — plugins (like the Cart2Cart service) import products, customers, and orders. Design and apps don't transfer. Our [migration guide](/tutorials/wordpress-hosting-migration) covers the move.

**Which is faster, WooCommerce or Shopify?**
With proper [WooCommerce hosting](/tutorials/woocommerce-hosting) you can beat Shopify — 200ms TTFB versus Shopify's typical 300–500ms. Shopify is more consistent out of the box; WooCommerce has the higher ceiling.

**What hosting do I need for WooCommerce?**
2+ CPU cores, 2GB+ RAM, NVMe, and server-side caching (Redis or LiteSpeed). Cloudways and Kinsta handle this out of the box. See [WooCommerce hosting requirements](/tutorials/woocommerce-hosting).

**Is Shopify good for SEO?**
Decent basics, limited advanced control. WordPress offers full schema, redirect, and speed control — a real advantage for content-heavy stores.
