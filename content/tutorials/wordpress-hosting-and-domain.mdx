## The one-line answer

**Register the domain at a dedicated registrar, host the website wherever you like.** Separate them. Here's why, and what happens when you bundle.

## Why people buy them together

- **One invoice** and one dashboard
- **Auto-setup** — the host connects your domain automatically
- **Free domain** on many plans (Hostinger, Bluehost, SiteGround include one for year one)

It's genuinely convenient — and completely fine *if you never switch hosts*.

## Why separating is the safer default

- **You control the domain** — if a host dispute, account issue, or host shutdown happens, your domain isn't collateral. The domain is your most valuable asset.
- **Domain renewal prices** are often cheaper at registrars (a `.com` is ~$10–12/yr at Namecheap/Cloudflare vs. premium at some hosts).
- **Free first-year domains renew at full price** — that "free" domain becomes $15–20/yr.
- **Switching hosts is 5 minutes** — change one A record instead of migrating a domain between registrars. See our [migration guide](/tutorials/wordpress-hosting-migration).

The horror story in one sentence: buy domain + hosting together, the host's billing glitches, your account is suspended, and your domain stops resolving — support doesn't care about your domain because they're a hosting company.

## Recommended setup

| Asset | Where | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Domain (`.com` etc.) | Namecheap, Cloudflare, Google Domains | Portable, cheap renewals, never hostage |
| WordPress hosting | Your chosen host | Pick on performance, not bundling |
| Email | Host email or Google Workspace | See [email setup](/tutorials/wordpress-email-setup) |

**Cloudflare** is a standout registrar choice for WordPress sites: it registers domains at cost (~$10/yr) and bundles free DNS, SSL, and CDN. Pair it with any host from the [best WordPress hosting](/best/wordpress-hosting) list.

## How to point your domain at your host

Once the domain is at the registrar and your site is live on the host (see [how to install WordPress](/tutorials/how-to-install-wordpress)):

1. **Get your host's nameservers or IP** — usually in the host panel under "Add Domain" or the welcome email
2. **At the registrar, set the A record** — `@` (or `yourdomain.com`) → your host's IP; add a `www` CNAME → `yourdomain.com`
3. **In your host panel, add the domain** — most hosts then auto-configure the site and SSL
4. **Propagation** — DNS changes take minutes to a few hours; don't panic if it's not instant

Some hosts give you nameservers instead of an IP — then you replace the registrar's nameservers with the host's (ns1/ns2). Either method works; nameservers are simpler, A records keep the registrar's DNS features (like Cloudflare's).

## Keep your domain safe

- **Enable registrar lock** (prevents unauthorized transfers)
- **Turn on two-factor authentication** at the registrar — domain theft is real
- **Use a payment method you monitor** — expiry is the #1 way people lose domains
- **Auto-renew** the domain even if you cancel hosting

## Frequently asked questions

**Is it better to buy hosting and domain together?**
For setup convenience, yes. For safety and long-term cost, no — register the domain at a dedicated registrar and keep hosting separate.

**Which domain registrar is best for WordPress?**
Namecheap and Cloudflare for cheap renewals and clean management. Cloudflare adds free DNS/SSL/CDN, which pairs well with [Cloudflare-based WordPress hosting](/tutorials/wordpress-hosting-cloudflare).

**What happens to my free domain if I change hosts?**
It stays at the old host (it's registered there), and you'd either transfer it or point it at the new host. Both are possible — transfer is cleaner. Our [migration guide](/tutorials/wordpress-hosting-migration) covers it.

**Can I use a domain I already own with any host?**
Yes — every host lets you point an external domain at your site. That's the setup above, and it's how the separate approach works in practice.

**Does Hostinger include a free domain?**
On many plans, for the first year. Renewals cost full price, and the domain lives with Hostinger — see our [Hostinger review](/reviews/hostinger-review) for the details.
