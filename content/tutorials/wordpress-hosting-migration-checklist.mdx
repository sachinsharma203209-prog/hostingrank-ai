## Before you start

A migration is only stressful if you skip the prep. The full how-to is in [WordPress migration](/tutorials/wordpress-hosting-migration) — this is the checklist that makes it safe. Tick every box.

### The pre-flight checklist

- [ ] **Back up** — full files + database, stored off-site, *tested*. [Backup guide](/tutorials/wordpress-backups)
- [ ] **Export email** — download mailboxes or switch to a new email provider first. [Email setup](/tutorials/wordpress-email-setup)
- [ ] **List your DNS records** — MX (email), SPF/DKIM/DMARC, subdomains, and any third-party services pointing at your domain. [Deliverability guide](/tutorials/wordpress-email-deliverability)
- [ ] **Check the new host's limits** — PHP version, storage, email, and whether your plugins are allowed
- [ ] **Pick a quiet window** — a low-traffic time; never mid-sale or mid-launch
- [ ] **Note your renewal/refund window** on the old host. [Refunds guide](/tutorials/wordpress-hosting-refunds)

## During the move

- [ ] **Move files + database** — via your host's free migration service (Kinsta, Cloudways, SiteGround, WP Engine all include one) or a migration plugin
- [ ] **Update URLs** — if the domain changed, update the Site Address and run a find-and-replace. The [move WordPress guide](/tutorials/move-wordpress-to-new-host) shows the safe method
- [ ] **Test on the new host before switching DNS** — load pages, log in, run a [speed test](/tutorials/wordpress-hosting-speed-testing), check forms and payment callbacks
- [ ] **Keep the old host live** — never cancel it until the new one is proven
- [ ] **Point the domain** — change nameservers or A records at your registrar. [Domains guide](/tutorials/wordpress-hosting-and-domain)

## After the switch

- [ ] **Wait for DNS propagation** — up to a few hours (usually minutes). Test from another network/device
- [ ] **Force HTTPS** — confirm the SSL provisioned and the padlock works on every page. [HTTPS guide](/tutorials/wordpress-hosting-and-https)
- [ ] **Test email** — MX records switched, SPF/DKIM updated, send a test to two addresses
- [ ] **Re-enable caching** — configure the new host's cache (LiteSpeed/Redis) and re-run the [speed guide](/tutorials/speed-up-wordpress)
- [ ] **Check integrations** — cron jobs, webhooks, payment callbacks, third-party services pointing at old IPs
- [ ] **Keep monitoring** — run [uptime monitoring](/tutorials/wordpress-hosting-uptime) for a week on the new host
- [ ] **Cancel the old host** — only after a week of stability, and within the [refund window](/tutorials/wordpress-hosting-refunds) if you can

## The three mistakes that break migrations

1. **Canceling the old host too early** — until DNS settles and you've tested, the old host is your safety net
2. **Forgetting DNS is more than A records** — MX and SPF/DKIM are how email breaks silently after a move
3. **Skipping the post-move test** — a site that "loads" can still have broken forms, images, or payment webhooks

## Frequently asked questions

**How long does a WordPress migration take?**
The move itself is 30–60 minutes; DNS propagation adds minutes to hours. Budget a quiet evening so you're not racing.

**What is the safest way to migrate WordPress?**
Host-provided free migration (managed hosts) or a backup plugin like All-in-One WP Migration — plus keeping the old host live until the new one is verified.

**Do I need to change DNS when changing hosts?**
Yes — point your domain at the new host (nameservers or A records). Email also moves if you're changing email providers — don't forget MX and SPF/DKIM. [Deliverability guide](/tutorials/wordpress-email-deliverability).

**Will migration affect my SEO?**
Not if you keep the same domain and HTTPS, and use proper redirects if URLs change. Downtime during the switch is the main risk — that's why you prepare. See [hosting and SEO](/tutorials/wordpress-hosting-and-seo).

**Can I migrate without downtime?**
Effectively yes — migrate the site, test it on the new host, then flip DNS. Visitors see no gap because both hosts are live during the switch.
