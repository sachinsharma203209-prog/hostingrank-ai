## The intro-price trap

Nearly every WordPress host advertises a headline price that **more than doubles at renewal**. It's not a scam — it's how the industry works. But it becomes a scam when nobody tells you the renewal number before you pay. This guide does: here's every major host's intro vs renewal price, so the second-year bill isn't a surprise.

| Host | Intro price | Renewal price | Billing term |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Namecheap | $1.58/mo | $4.48/mo | 24-month lock-in |
| Hostinger | $2.99/mo | $7.99/mo | 48-month intro |
| Bluehost | $2.95/mo | $11.99/mo | 36-month intro |
| SiteGround | $3.99/mo | $14.99/mo | Annual |
| DreamHost | $2.59/mo | $8.99/mo | 36-month intro |
| A2 Hosting | $2.99/mo | $9.99/mo | 36-month intro |
| HostGator | $2.75/mo | $10.99/mo | 36-month intro |

Renewals are usually annual or per your original term. The gap between $2.99 and $7.99 is the real question you should ask **before** buying, not after a year of building on the host.

## Why hosts do this

The economics are simple: hosts use cheap intros to win the switch, then recover margin at renewal. On long intro terms (24–48 months), the renewal is even more expensive per month because you pre-paid cheap. Read the buying rules in [cheap WordPress hosting](/tutorials/cheap-wordpress-hosting) before committing.

## The honest hosts

A few providers break the pattern:

- **Cloudways** — no intro games; pay-as-you-go, month to month (from ~$14/mo)
- **Kinsta / WP Engine** — flat, published pricing with no intro/renewal gap
- **Namecheap** — renewals stay low, just higher than the intro

If price predictability matters more than the cheapest first month, managed or month-to-month options are the honest choice — see [managed WordPress hosting](/tutorials/managed-wordpress-hosting).

## How to protect yourself

1. **Find the renewal price before checkout** — it's usually buried in the terms or a footnote
2. **Treat multi-year intros as a bet** — a 4-year Hostinger plan at $2.99/mo is great *if* you stay 4 years; if you leave, unused months are lost
3. **Set a calendar reminder** — note the renewal date and review whether to stay or switch (see [when to upgrade or switch](/tutorials/wordpress-hosting-when-to-upgrade))
4. **Know your refund window** — if a renewal surprises you, the guarantee may cover it: [refunds guide](/tutorials/wordpress-hosting-refunds)
5. **Migrate instead of paying** — switching hosts is a 30-minute job with a [migration](/tutorials/wordpress-hosting-migration), and intro prices apply to new customers

## Frequently asked questions

**Why does my hosting cost more at renewal?**
Intro prices exist to win new customers; renewals return to the normal rate. The gap is industry-standard — the only variable is how big it is and how honest they are about it.

**Which WordPress host has the lowest renewal price?**
Namecheap renews lowest among shared hosts. For no renewal games at all, Cloudways and the managed hosts (Kinsta, WP Engine) are month-to-month and flat-priced.

**Can I get the intro price again at renewal?**
Sometimes — many hosts offer "renewal discounts" if you ask, or if you migrate away and back. Politely asking support or switching via a [migration](/tutorials/wordpress-hosting-migration) both work.

**Should I pay for 4 years of hosting upfront?**
Only if you're sure you'll stay. Multi-year intros are cheap but non-refundable for unused months. One-year terms keep your options open.

**How do I avoid the renewal price increase?**
Set a reminder before renewal, compare the renewal against the [best WordPress hosting](/best/wordpress-hosting) list, and switch with a migration if it's not worth it.
