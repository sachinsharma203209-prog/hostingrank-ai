## The most confusing question in WordPress

**WordPress.com** and **WordPress.org** share a name but are completely different products:

- **WordPress.org** is the open-source WordPress software you install on your own hosting. You own everything — themes, plugins, code, data.
- **WordPress.com** is a hosted platform run by Automattic (the company behind WordPress). You rent a managed blog; WordPress.com handles hosting, security, and updates.

This guide is about that choice, and how it interacts with **WordPress hosting**.

## WordPress.com vs WordPress.org side by side

| | WordPress.org (self-hosted) | WordPress.com (hosted) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cost | Hosting $2–35/mo | Free to $45/mo plans |
| Plugins | All of them | Limited by plan |
| Themes | All of them | Limited by plan |
| Code access | Full (FTP/SSH) | None |
| Ads | No forced ads | On free plan |
| Domain | Your own | Subdomain on free |
| Ownership | Yours, exportable | Yours, exportable |

## WordPress.org: what you get with real hosting

Self-hosted WordPress + a [WordPress host](/tutorials/what-is-wordpress-hosting) gives you:

- **Every plugin and theme** — WooCommerce, page builders, SEO tools, anything
- **Full control** — code, database, cron jobs, custom stacks
- **No forced ads** — your site, your monetization
- **Portable** — move hosts anytime with a migration (see our [migration guide](/tutorials/wordpress-hosting-migration))
- **Cost control** — $2.99/mo hosting beats WordPress.com's plugin-enabled tiers

The catch: you're responsible for updates, security, and backups. That's the maintenance work covered in [WordPress hosting and maintenance](/tutorials/wordpress-hosting-maintenance).

## WordPress.com: what the hosted plan buys

WordPress.com's appeal is **zero maintenance**:

- **Free plan** — blog on a subdomain with ads and 1GB storage
- **Personal ($4/mo)** — custom domain, no ads, 6GB
- **Premium ($8/mo)** — some design customization
- **Business ($25/mo)** — plugins and custom themes (the plan that finally matches self-hosted capability)
- **Commerce ($45/mo)** — WooCommerce-ready

Note the pricing trap: WordPress.com only unlocks plugins at the **$25/mo Business tier** — where self-hosted WordPress already costs $3–14/mo with the same capabilities.

## Which should you choose?

**Choose WordPress.org (self-hosted):**
- You want plugins like WooCommerce, SEO tools, or page builders
- You want to control and own your site long-term
- You're willing to spend an hour a month on updates (or pay for managed hosting)
- You want cheap, flexible hosting — see [best WordPress hosting](/best/wordpress-hosting)

**Choose WordPress.com:**
- You want zero maintenance, period
- You're writing a simple blog and don't need plugins
- You're happy within their platform limits
- You want to test WordPress before committing

## The verdict

For almost every real website — business, store, blog with growth plans — **self-hosted WordPress on your own hosting wins**. It's cheaper at equivalent capability, fully portable, and unconstrained. WordPress.com makes sense only for casual blogging or people who refuse to touch anything technical.

If you go self-hosted, start with the [WordPress hosting for beginners](/tutorials/wordpress-hosting-for-beginners) guide — it takes about 15 minutes.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is WordPress.com free?**
Yes, with limits — subdomain, forced ads, 1GB storage, no plugins or custom themes. Paid plans from $4/mo remove limits progressively.

**Can I use plugins on WordPress.com?**
Only on the Business ($25/mo) and Commerce ($45/mo) plans. On self-hosted WordPress.org, every plugin is available for the cost of hosting.

**Is WordPress.com the same as WordPress hosting?**
No. WordPress.com is a closed hosted platform. WordPress hosting (this site's topic) runs the open-source WordPress.org software with full control.

**Can I move from WordPress.com to self-hosted WordPress?**
Yes — WordPress.com has a one-click export, and your new host or a plugin imports it. Our [migration guide](/tutorials/wordpress-hosting-migration) covers the process.

**Which is better for a small business?**
Self-hosted WordPress on a reliable host — more features, full ownership, and lower cost at equivalent capability. See [best WordPress hosting](/best/wordpress-hosting).
