## What uptime actually means

Uptime is the percentage of time your site is available. The math most people miss:

| Uptime | Downtime per year |
| --- | --- |
| 99.9% | ~8.7 hours |
| 99.95% | ~4.4 hours |
| 99.98% | ~1.8 hours |
| 99.99% | ~53 minutes |
| 100% | Perfect (rare) |

Every "9" sounds the same but is 10x the reliability. A **99.9% guarantee** means your store can be down for nearly nine hours a year — for an ecommerce site, that's real revenue. Our tests log actual uptime for every host on the [benchmarks](/benchmarks) page.

## Why hosts quote the numbers

Most hosts advertise **99.9% uptime** because it's the industry baseline and easy to defend. A few things to know:

- **Guarantees aren't reality** — the SLA is what they'll compensate you for, not what you'll experience. Kinsta guarantees 100% (credits if exceeded); most others cap at 99.9%
- **Maintenance windows** are usually excluded
- **"Unlimited" plans** still have resource caps — oversold servers cause the "site up but loading for 30 seconds" failure that uptime monitors miss

The number to care about isn't the advertised guarantee — it's the **measured** uptime from real monitoring. That's what our provider [reviews](/reviews) and the [best WordPress hosting](/best/wordpress-hosting) rankings use.

## How to monitor your uptime

Your host's word isn't proof — set up independent monitoring:

- **UptimeRobot** — free, checks every minute, email/SMS alerts
- **Pingdom / Better Stack** — richer reports and status pages
- **Host-integrated** — Kinsta and Cloudways include uptime alerts

Good practice:

1. Monitor at least **two locations** (your audience's regions)
2. Alert on **load time too** — a site that responds in 30s is down in practice
3. Log **downtime history** — this is the evidence if you ever claim an SLA credit
4. Tie monitoring into your [maintenance routine](/tutorials/wordpress-hosting-maintenance)

## Frequently asked questions

**Is 99.9% uptime good for WordPress?**
It's the baseline — about 8.7 hours of downtime a year. For a business site, aim for hosts that measure 99.98%+ in practice.

**What does a 100% uptime SLA mean?**
Kinsta's guarantee: if your site drops below 100%, you get credit. It's the strongest SLA in the industry and they honor it. See the [Kinsta review](/reviews/kinsta-review).

**How do I check my host's real uptime?**
Run UptimeRobot or Better Stack for 30 days. One-month data beats a five-year guarantee.

**Do shared hosts have worse uptime than managed?**
On average, yes — oversold servers and less infrastructure redundancy. But quality shared hosts (Hostinger, SiteGround) still measured 99.98–99.99% in our tests.

**What is the best WordPress hosting for uptime?**
Kinsta (100% SLA), then Cloudways and WP Engine (99.99%+). For budget, Hostinger and SiteGround measured best. Rankings: [best WordPress hosting](/best/wordpress-hosting).
