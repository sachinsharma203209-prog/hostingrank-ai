## Why WordPress email fails

By default, WordPress sends mail through PHP's `mail()` function — which has no authentication, no retry, and a terrible spam reputation. Result: password resets, WooCommerce order notifications, and contact form submissions silently vanish into spam folders.

The fix is **SMTP**: routing WordPress mail through a real mail server that authenticates with your domain. This guide covers both options:

- **A transactional service** (Mailgun, SendGrid, Postmark) — best for anything with real volume
- **Your host's SMTP server** — fine for low-volume site mail on shared hosting

## Step 1: Choose an SMTP service

| Service | Free tier | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mailgun | 100 emails/day | General transactional mail |
| SendGrid | 100 emails/day | High volume, Deliverability reports |
| Postmark | 100 emails/month | Order/receipt email, max deliverability |
| Host SMTP | Free with plan | Tiny volumes on shared hosting |

For a typical small business site, **any of these works** — pick the one with the clearest dashboard. If you already have [domain email](/tutorials/wordpress-email-setup) on your host, you can also use the host's SMTP server (often `smtp.yourhost.com`, port 465) with a mailbox account.

## Step 2: Install and configure an SMTP plugin

The standard is **WP Mail SMTP** (free version covers one mailer):

1. Install **WP Mail SMTP** from the WordPress plugin directory and activate
2. Go to **WP Mail SMTP → Settings**
3. Choose your mailer (Mailgun, SendGrid, Postmark, or **Other SMTP** for your host)
4. Paste the API key or credentials from your provider
5. Set **From Email** to a real address on your domain
6. Save

If you chose **Other SMTP**, you'll also set:
- SMTP host (e.g. `smtp.mailgun.org`) and port (`587` or `465`)
- Encryption (**TLS** for 587, **SSL** for 465)
- Authentication + your SMTP username/password

## Step 3: Send a test email

WP Mail SMTP includes a test tool under **Settings → Email Test**:

1. Enter any address and send
2. A successful send shows a green checkmark and the mailer used
3. If it fails, the error message names the problem — usually a wrong port, missing TLS, or a bad key

Confirm the test mail actually arrives (not in spam), then also test from your WooCommerce/contact form to see the real message path.

## Step 4: Verify authentication

Transactional services give you SPF and DKIM records to publish for your **sending domain** (see [email deliverability](/tutorials/wordpress-email-deliverability)). For Mailgun, publish their MX record too, so bounces return to them instead of failing. After DNS propagates, the service dashboard shows "verified" and your deliverability score rises.

Then **warm up**: start at low volume and scale over 1–2 weeks if your domain is new. Ramping gradually is what keeps inboxes from filtering you.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why are my WordPress emails going to spam?**
Almost always because of PHP `mail()` (no authentication) or missing SPF/DKIM. Switching to SMTP with an authenticated service fixes both.

**Is WP Mail SMTP free?**
Yes — the free version supports one mailer and is enough for most sites. The Pro version adds multiple mailers, white-labeling, and backup connections.

**Can I use my host's SMTP server?**
Yes, on shared hosting (Hostinger, SiteGround, Bluehost) use the host's SMTP hostname with a mailbox account. It works for low volume; use a transactional service if you send more.

**Mailgun or SendGrid or Postmark for WordPress?**
All three. Mailgun and SendGrid suit general volume; Postmark is best for transactional-only sites (orders, receipts) with strict deliverability needs.

**Does SMTP work with WooCommerce emails?**
Yes — WooCommerce uses WordPress's mailer, so once SMTP is configured every order email goes through it. Our [WooCommerce hosting](/tutorials/woocommerce-hosting) guide covers the full store setup.
