## Why you need a custom domain email

`you@yourdomain.com` is the professional baseline. It builds trust with customers, and it delivers better than free addresses — Gmail and Outlook treat mail from free domains more aggressively. The good news: it's cheap or free, and it works alongside your [WordPress hosting](/tutorials/what-is-wordpress-hosting).

You have two paths:

1. **Host-included email** — free with most shared plans (Hostinger, SiteGround, Bluehost, A2)
2. **Dedicated email service** — Google Workspace, Microsoft 365, or Zoho from $0–6/mo

## Host email vs Google Workspace vs Zoho

| Option | Cost | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Host-included email | Free | A few inboxes, light use |
| Zoho Mail | Free (5 mailboxes) → $1/mo | Budget professional email |
| Google Workspace | $6/user/mo | Businesses that live in Gmail |
| Microsoft 365 | $6/user/mo | Outlook-centric teams |

**Our rule of thumb:** start with host-included email for a personal site or side project. Switch to Google Workspace or Zoho when email becomes critical — a business domain that can't lose mail. See [WordPress hosting and email](/tutorials/wordpress-hosting-and-email) for the full trade-off.

## Step 1: Decide where your email lives

If you're on a shared host, your plan almost certainly includes mailboxes — check your control panel before paying for anything. The detailed setup for host mailboxes is in our [email & SSL setup](/tutorials/setup-email-ssl) guide.

If you choose a dedicated service:

- **Google Workspace** — sign up at workspace.google.com with your domain
- **Zoho Mail** — zoho.com/mail, verify your domain and add users free
- **Microsoft 365** — admin.microsoft.com, add your domain under Setup

## Step 2: Create the mailbox

**On your host:** control panel → **Email → Email Accounts** → add `you@yourdomain.com`, set a password.

**On Google Workspace/Zoho:** create the user in the admin console and assign the mailbox license. The service will prompt you to verify domain ownership with a TXT record.

## Step 3: Update MX and auth records

This is the step that makes email actually work. In your DNS panel (usually at your domain registrar or host):

1. **MX records** — point at your email provider (your provider's docs list the exact values and priority)
2. **SPF** — a TXT record declaring which servers may send for your domain
3. **DKIM** — your provider gives you a key to publish; this signs your outgoing mail
4. **DMARC** — tells receiving servers what to do with mail that fails SPF/DKIM

Get all three right and your mail won't land in spam. The full walkthrough is in [email deliverability](/tutorials/wordpress-email-deliverability).

## Step 4: Connect your mail client

Add the account to the app you actually use:

- **Gmail** → Settings → Accounts → **Add another email address** (or "Check mail from other accounts")
- **Outlook** → File → Add Account
- **Apple Mail** → Mail → Settings → Accounts → Add

Use the provider's IMAP/SMTP settings (typically `imap.provider.com` and `smtp.provider.com`, ports 993/465 with SSL). When in doubt, the provider's setup page auto-configures most clients.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I set up email without hosting?**
Yes — buy the domain anywhere and point its MX records at Google Workspace, Zoho, or Microsoft 365. No web hosting required.

**What is the cheapest professional email for my domain?**
Zoho's free tier covers up to 5 mailboxes with your own domain. Google Workspace is $6/user/mo.

**Do I need a separate email host if I use Hostinger?**
Hostinger's shared plans include email, so no. Their own email product is a good middle ground if you want more reliability than shared mail.

**Why is my custom email going to spam?**
Usually missing SPF/DKIM/DMARC, or a brand-new domain sending too much too fast. Fix the records, then send small volumes and warm up. Full guide: [email deliverability](/tutorials/wordpress-email-deliverability).

**What about transactional mail from my WordPress site?**
Password resets and order notifications should go through an SMTP service, not your mailbox. See [WordPress SMTP setup](/tutorials/wordpress-email-smtp).
