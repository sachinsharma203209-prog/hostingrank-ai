## What is WordPress hosting?

WordPress hosting is web hosting optimized to run WordPress — fast servers, one-click installs, free SSL, and often WordPress-specific caching and security. It's the same idea as any [web hosting](/tutorials/wordpress-hosting-vs-web-hosting), but tuned for the world's most popular CMS. The full beginner explanation is in [what is WordPress hosting](/tutorials/what-is-wordpress-hosting).

## How much does WordPress hosting cost?

- **Shared hosting:** $2.99–5/mo (intro) — the standard for most sites
- **Managed WordPress:** $14–35/mo — convenience, staging, better performance
- **VPS/cloud:** $6–30/mo — dedicated resources for growing sites

Watch the **renewal prices** — intros are cheap, renewals are the real cost. Full numbers: [renewal prices](/tutorials/wordpress-hosting-renewal-prices) and [cheap WordPress hosting](/tutorials/cheap-wordpress-hosting).

## Which WordPress hosting is best?

The short answer from our 6-month tests: **Hostinger for value, Cloudways for managed flexibility, Kinsta for premium performance.** The full data is in [best WordPress hosting](/best/wordpress-hosting) and every [provider review](/reviews).

## What is the difference between shared and managed hosting?

- **Shared** — your site shares a server's resources with others; cheap, simple, fine for most sites
- **Managed** — dedicated resources plus the host handles updates, caching, security, and support

Detailed comparison: [shared vs managed WordPress hosting](/tutorials/shared-vs-managed-wordpress-hosting) and [managed WordPress hosting](/tutorials/managed-wordpress-hosting).

## How fast should WordPress hosting be?

Aim for **TTFB under 200ms** from your audience's region and **99.9%+ uptime**. Our benchmarks show the good hosts (Kinsta 98ms, Cloudways 124ms, Hostinger 142ms) vs the slow ones (400–700ms). See [benchmarks](/benchmarks) and [how to test speed](/tutorials/wordpress-hosting-speed-testing).

## Is cheap hosting reliable?

Cheap and reliable aren't opposites — Hostinger at $2.99/mo delivered 99.99% uptime and 142ms TTFB in our tests. What's unreliable is *oversold* cheap hosting. The [best cheap hosting](/best/cheap-hosting) list separates the two.

## Can I get free WordPress hosting?

Yes, but free tiers mean ads, a subdomain, no email, and weak performance — fine for learning, not for a real site. The honest options and their limits: [free WordPress hosting](/tutorials/free-wordpress-hosting).

## Is WordPress.org hosting free?

The **software** is free. You pay for hosting ($3/mo+) and a domain (~$10/yr). WordPress.com (the hosted version) has a free tier with heavy limits. The difference is explained in [WordPress.com vs WordPress.org](/tutorials/wordpress-com-vs-wordpress-org).

## How do I install WordPress?

One-click installers on every major host, or manually in ~15 minutes. Complete walkthrough: [how to install WordPress](/tutorials/how-to-install-wordpress).

## How do I move my WordPress site to a new host?

With your host's free migration service or a migration plugin — typically 30 minutes. Full process: [WordPress migration](/tutorials/wordpress-hosting-migration).

## Does hosting matter for SEO?

Yes — TTFB, Core Web Vitals, uptime, and HTTPS are all ranking factors your host controls. How to leverage that: [WordPress hosting and SEO](/tutorials/wordpress-hosting-and-seo).

## How do I keep WordPress secure?

Strong passwords, 2FA, updates, HTTPS, a WAF, and daily backups. The full 9-point checklist: [WordPress hosting security](/tutorials/wordpress-hosting-security).

## How do I make WordPress faster?

Caching, Redis, PHP 8.3, a CDN, and image optimization — measured, not guessed. The complete guide: [speed up WordPress](/tutorials/speed-up-wordpress).

## Do I need WordPress-specific hosting, or does any host work?

Any host with PHP and MySQL runs WordPress. But "runs it" and "runs it fast" differ enormously — which is why the [host rankings](/best/wordpress-hosting) exist. If you're deciding between WordPress and another platform, start with [WordPress vs site builders](/tutorials/wordpress-hosting-vs-site-builders).
