## What makes ecommerce hosting different

An online store is the highest-stakes kind of WordPress site: it takes payments, holds customer data, and dies at the worst possible moment — during a sale. Ecommerce hosting has three non-negotiables the blog world skips:

1. **Reliability** — downtime is lost revenue, literally
2. **Security & compliance** — cardholder data demands HTTPS, patching, and often PCI-DSS-friendly setup
3. **Performance under load** — cart sessions, product queries, and traffic spikes strain shared plans

If you're deciding between WordPress and a platform, see [WordPress vs Shopify](/tutorials/wordpress-hosting-vs-shopify) first. This guide is for the WooCommerce-and-beyond WordPress route.

## The five requirements

### 1. Enough CPU and RAM
WooCommerce fires many more database queries than a blog — every product page, cart check, and search. Minimum: **2 CPU cores, 2GB RAM**. Entry shared plans throttle real stores.

### 2. Server-side caching
Redis or LiteSpeed caching is what keeps repeat visits fast. It's built into managed plans (Cloudways, Kinsta) and available on Hostinger's LiteSpeed stack. See [speed up WordPress](/tutorials/speed-up-wordpress) for setup.

### 3. SSL and payment compliance
Free SSL is standard on every host we rank — enable it and force HTTPS ([guide](/tutorials/setup-email-ssl)). Payment gateways (Stripe, PayPal) handle the bulk of PCI scope, but your host must be patched and current — that's where [security](/tutorials/wordpress-hosting-security) matters.

### 4. Daily backups
An order database is irreplaceable. Daily, off-site [backups](/tutorials/wordpress-backups) are non-negotiable — a checkout table gone is revenue gone.

### 5. Staging
Test plugin and theme updates before they touch production. Managed hosts include one-click [staging](/tutorials/wordpress-staging-environment); shared hosts need a plugin.

## When ecommerce outgrows shared hosting

Signals to move up (details in [when to upgrade hosting](/tutorials/wordpress-hosting-when-to-upgrade)):

- Payment callbacks timing out during traffic spikes
- Cart sessions dropping under load
- Resource warnings from your host
- Checkout pages taking over 3 seconds

The upgrade path is managed WordPress or a managed VPS — both handle stores that outgrew shared plans. Our [VPS hosting guide](/tutorials/wordpress-vps-hosting) covers the raw route.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the best WordPress hosting for an online store?**
Cloudways for value, Kinsta for premium performance, Hostinger Business for budget. All three are compared in the [WooCommerce hosting guide](/tutorials/woocommerce-hosting).

**Does ecommerce hosting need to be PCI-DSS compliant?**
The host keeps servers patched and secure; the payment gateway handles most PCI scope on your side. Use Stripe/PayPal and keep WordPress updated — see [security](/tutorials/wordpress-hosting-security).

**How much should an online store pay for hosting?**
Budget stores can start around $5/mo (Hostinger Business). Serious stores should plan $14–35/mo for managed resources that handle checkouts under load.

**Is shared hosting enough for WooCommerce?**
For a small store, yes. As products and traffic grow, the CPU/RAM limits bite — that's when you move to managed or VPS. See [WordPress vs Shopify](/tutorials/wordpress-hosting-vs-shopify) for the platform question.

**How do I keep a store fast?**
Redis object caching, a CDN for product images, image compression, and regular [database optimization](/tutorials/wordpress-hosting-database-optimization). Run the full [speed guide](/tutorials/speed-up-wordpress) after setup.
