## Hosting is only half the job

Buying WordPress hosting gets you a server. **Maintenance** is everything that keeps that site fast, secure, and online — and most of it is not the host's job. A neglected WordPress site gets slower, vulnerable, and eventually hacked regardless of how good the host is.

Here's the complete maintenance checklist, what you can automate, and when to outsource.

## The weekly maintenance checklist

### 1. Updates
- **WordPress core** — always update (minor updates auto-apply)
- **Plugins** — update weekly; test on staging first if the plugin is critical
- **Themes** — update and remove unused themes entirely (they're an attack vector)

### 2. Backups
- **Enable the host's backups** — most hosts include daily or weekly
- **Add an off-site backup** — UpdraftPlus to Google Drive/Dropbox, so one host outage can't lose your data
- **Test a restore** — quarterly, restore a backup on a staging site to confirm it works

### 3. Security
- **Use strong admin passwords** and 2FA on `wp-admin`
- **Limit login attempts** — free plugins (Limit Login Attempts, Wordfence)
- **Keep plugins minimal** — every extra plugin is attack surface
- **Enable a WAF/CDN** — Cloudflare free tier or your host's WAF
- **Scan for malware** — Wordfence or your host's scanner, monthly

### 4. Performance
- **Monitor TTFB and LCP** — our [speed up WordPress](/tutorials/speed-up-wordpress) guide covers measurement
- **Audit plugins** — deactivate anything unused; plugin count is the #1 performance killer
- **Check caching** — page cache, object cache (Redis), and image optimization
- **Review the database** — delete spam, transients, and old revisions monthly

### 5. Uptime monitoring
- **Set up uptime alerts** — free (UptimeRobot, Better Stack)
- **Check the host's status page** for maintenance windows
- **Keep contact info current** — you need the host's alerts to reach you

## Maintenance plans by hosting type

| Hosting | You handle | Host handles |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shared (Hostinger, Bluehost) | Updates, security, backups | Server uptime, hardware |
| Managed (Kinsta, WP Engine) | Content, plugins (mostly) | Updates, security, backups, speed |
| VPS/Cloud (DO, AWS) | Everything | Bare infrastructure |

Managed hosts remove most of the work — that's the value in the [managed WordPress hosting](/tutorials/managed-wordpress-hosting) guide. On raw cloud ([AWS](/tutorials/wordpress-hosting-aws), DigitalOcean), you're the entire IT department.

## When to outsource WordPress maintenance

Consider a **maintenance service** (many agencies bundle them with hosting) when:

- You don't have time for weekly updates
- Your site handles payments or customer data
- An outage or breach would hurt your business
- You want someone accountable for uptime

Typical pricing: **$50–200/mo** for updates + backups + monitoring + a monthly security/performance check. Compare that to the cost of one hacked site.

## The maintenance schedule at a glance

- **Daily** — automatic backups (host)
- **Weekly** — plugin/theme updates, uptime check
- **Monthly** — security scan, database cleanup, performance review
- **Quarterly** — test a backup restore, audit plugins and user accounts
- **Annually** — reassess your host and plan; see [best WordPress hosting](/best/wordpress-hosting) when you do

## Frequently asked questions

**What is WordPress hosting and maintenance?**
WordPress hosting is the server your site runs on. WordPress maintenance is the ongoing work — updates, backups, security, performance — that keeps it fast and safe. Many agencies bundle both as a monthly service.

**How much does WordPress maintenance cost?**
DIY: your time. Managed hosting: $20–35/mo includes most of it. Full maintenance services: $50–200/mo for updates, backups, monitoring, and support.

**Is WordPress maintenance necessary?**
Yes — outdated WordPress sites are the most common hacking target, and neglected sites slow down. The minimum safe routine is updates, backups, and login security.

**Does managed WordPress hosting include maintenance?**
Mostly — Kinsta and WP Engine handle updates, security, backups, and performance as part of the plan. Content and plugin choices stay yours. See [managed WordPress hosting](/tutorials/managed-wordpress-hosting).

**How do I start a maintenance routine?**
Enable host backups, set up uptime alerts, update everything, and run our [speed-up WordPress](/tutorials/speed-up-wordpress) checklist once. That covers 80% of the value.
