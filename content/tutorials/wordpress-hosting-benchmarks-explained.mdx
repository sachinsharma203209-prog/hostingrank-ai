## Why benchmarks matter

Every host claims to be fast. Benchmarks are the difference between marketing and measurement — real numbers from real tests. This site's [benchmarks](/benchmarks) page and every [provider review](/reviews) are built from one method: a fresh WordPress install on each host, tested over 30+ days. This guide explains what each number means and how to read them.

## The metrics, decoded

### TTFB (time to first byte)
The time from request until the server's first byte arrives. **The most host-specific metric** — caching and front-end tricks barely touch it.

- Under 200ms: excellent (Kinsta 98ms, Cloudways 124ms, Hostinger 142ms)
- 200–400ms: acceptable
- 400ms+: slow — a hosting problem

### Load time
The time until the page finishes loading. Visitor-facing, and affected by the front end as much as the server. The gap between TTFB and load time is your theme, images, and scripts — fixable with the [speed guide](/tutorials/speed-up-wordpress).

### Uptime
Percentage of time the site was reachable. 99.99% ≈ 53 min/year down; 99.9% ≈ 8.7 hours/year. The numbers live in the [uptime guide](/tutorials/wordpress-hosting-uptime).

### Performance score
Our composite of TTFB, load time, and Core Web Vitals — a single 0–100 view. A score above 90 means the host is genuinely fast in practice.

### Latency by region
TTFB measured per test location (India, US, UK). This reveals **location dependence** — a host that's 98ms from US-East may be 250ms from Mumbai. The [data center guide](/tutorials/wordpress-hosting-data-centers) explains the why.

## Our test method (reproduce it yourself)

1. **Fresh WordPress install** — default theme, no optimization, so the numbers reflect the host alone
2. **12 global test locations** — India, US, UK, and more
3. **3 runs each, median reported** — median kills outlier spikes
4. **30+ days continuous** — uptime needs a month to be meaningful
5. **Re-test after traffic spikes** — to catch throttling

The full methodology is published on the [methodology](/methodology) page. You can run the same test on any host with our [speed testing guide](/tutorials/wordpress-hosting-speed-testing).

## How to use the numbers

**Comparing hosts:** don't compare different numbers from different reviewers — compare hosts tested the *same way* on the same page. Our [benchmarks](/benchmarks) table does exactly that.

**Choosing a host:**
- TTFB under 200ms from *your* audience's region (see [data centers](/tutorials/wordpress-hosting-data-centers))
- Uptime 99.9%+ (99.99% is the managed tier's norm)
- Performance score above 85–90

**Noting the caveats:**
- Median, not average — averages hide the spike behavior
- Default setup, not your optimized site — your results will differ after [optimization](/tutorials/speed-up-wordpress)
- Region-dependent — always read your region's latency, not just the global average

## Frequently asked questions

**What is a good TTFB for WordPress?**
Under 200ms from your audience's region. 200–400ms is acceptable; above 400ms, the host is the bottleneck.

**What does a performance score of 95 mean?**
A composite of speed metrics — 90+ means the host performs like the premium tier in practice, not just on paper.

**How is uptime measured in benchmarks?**
Continuous probes from multiple locations over 30+ days. One-day measurements are meaningless — that's why our tests run for a month. See [uptime guide](/tutorials/wordpress-hosting-uptime).

**Why is my site slower than the benchmark?**
Benchmarks use a default WordPress install. Your theme, plugins, and images add load — run the [speed guide](/tutorials/speed-up-wordpress) to close the gap.

**How do I benchmark my own host?**
Use the same method: fresh install, 12 locations, 3 runs each, median. The full steps are in [how to test hosting speed](/tutorials/wordpress-hosting-speed-testing).
