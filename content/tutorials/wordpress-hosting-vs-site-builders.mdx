## The two ways to build a website

Almost every website decision comes down to two routes:

1. **Self-hosted WordPress** — open-source software you install on hosting you control
2. **A website builder (Wix, Squarespace)** — an all-in-one platform where the builder is the hosting

WordPress.com sits in between as a hosted version of WordPress — we compare that in [WordPress.com vs WordPress.org](/tutorials/wordpress-com-vs-wordpress-org). This guide is about builders versus real WordPress hosting.

## Cost: builders are subscription-priced

| | WordPress + hosting | Wix | Squarespace |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Entry cost | $2.99/mo hosting + ~$10/yr domain | $17/mo (annual) | $16/mo (annual) |
| What you own | Everything, exportable | Nothing exportable cleanly | Nothing exportable cleanly |
| Renewals | Host $3–6/mo, domain $10–15/yr | $27/mo+ after year 1 | $23–27/mo after year 1 |

Over three years, self-hosted WordPress typically costs **$150–250 total**; Wix or Squarespace cost **$600–1,000+**. Builders aren't free — they're priced like software subscriptions, and the cheapest "with ads" tiers still cost more than hosting.

## Control and lock-in

**WordPress + hosting:**
- Every plugin, theme, and code change is yours
- Full access to files, database, and server settings (cPanel/hPanel)
- You can [migrate to any host](/tutorials/wordpress-hosting-migration) anytime
- Your site is portable; you own the data

**Builders:**
- You work inside the platform's editor and widget set
- Export options are limited (a static HTML copy at best, no plugins)
- Your content is effectively trapped in the platform
- Pricing changes are the platform's call

For anything you may want to grow or move later, portability is the difference between owning a website and renting one.

## SEO and performance

- **WordPress**: full SEO control — your host's TTFB, caching, and CDN are yours to tune. The [best WordPress hosting](/best/wordpress-hosting) plans hit sub-200ms TTFB with LiteSpeed and NVMe.
- **Wix**: decent built-in SEO, but you can't control server-level performance; TTFB is often 400–700ms.
- **Squarespace**: clean code and decent SEO defaults, but again no server control and slower average load times.

Long-tail SEO — schema, redirects, custom taxonomies, page speed fixes — is dramatically easier on WordPress.

## Ease of use

Builders win the first hour: drag-and-drop, no setup, nothing to install. WordPress takes a few hours to feel comfortable — but you install it in minutes with one-click installers (see [how to install WordPress](/tutorials/how-to-install-wordpress)) and every host in our rankings does the hard parts for you.

For most non-technical users, WordPress + a page builder (Elementor, Gutenberg) closes most of the convenience gap while keeping the control.

## The verdict

- **Choose WordPress hosting** if you want to own your site, control cost, scale without lock-in, or plan to earn money from it (SEO, ecommerce, ads).
- **Choose a builder** if you need a site live this afternoon and will never want to move, extend, or optimize it beyond the platform.

Builders are a fine rental. WordPress is ownership. For anything with business ambitions — small business, blog, or store — self-hosted WordPress on [budget hosting](/tutorials/cheap-wordpress-hosting) is the long-term cheaper, better answer.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is WordPress cheaper than Wix and Squarespace?**
Yes — significantly over 2–3 years. WordPress hosting starts at $2.99/mo versus $16–17/mo, and you keep everything you build.

**Is WordPress harder than Wix?**
The first session is harder (a few more steps), but one-click installs and page builders close the gap fast. Ongoing maintenance is roughly an hour a month — see [WordPress maintenance](/tutorials/wordpress-hosting-maintenance).

**Can I move from Wix to WordPress?**
You can migrate content (posts/pages/images) and rebuild the design. Plugins, apps, and the exact layout don't transfer. Our [migration guide](/tutorials/wordpress-hosting-migration) covers importing content.

**Which is better for SEO, WordPress or Wix?**
WordPress, especially for long-tail and technical SEO — full control over speed (host), schema, redirects, and structure. Builders are fine for basics.

**What about WordPress.com?**
WordPress.com is the hosted middle ground — easier than self-hosted, but with plan limits (plugins only at $25/mo). Full breakdown: [WordPress.com vs WordPress.org](/tutorials/wordpress-com-vs-wordpress-org).
