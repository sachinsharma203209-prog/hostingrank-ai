## Cheap WordPress hosting done right

"Cheap WordPress hosting" usually means shared hosting under $5/mo — and the trick is knowing what the advertised price actually buys you. Here's what we found testing ten budget providers for a full quarter:

- **Intro prices** are real, but renewals run 76–151% higher
- **NVMe + LiteSpeed** hosts are dramatically faster than old Apache stacks
- **Resource limits** matter more than advertised "unlimited" bandwidth

The good news: you don't need to spend $35/mo on managed hosting for most sites. The best cheap WordPress hosting is genuinely excellent.

## The best cheap WordPress hosting picks

### 1. Hostinger — best overall cheap plan
Hostinger's Single plan starts at **$2.99/mo** (₹69/mo over 48 months in India). Despite the price, our tests measured a **142ms global TTFB** and **99.99% uptime** — numbers most "premium" hosts can't match. The stack is LiteSpeed + NVMe with a free SSL, daily backups, and a one-click WordPress installer. Just know the renewal is higher and the cheapest tier has no free domain. Full details in the [Hostinger review](/reviews/hostinger-review).

### 2. Namecheap — best value with a domain
Namecheap starts at $1.58/mo and includes a free domain on most plans, making it the cheapest overall if you need a domain anyway. TTFB around 340ms is fine for the price, and renewal pricing is more transparent than most. Read the [Namecheap review](/reviews/namecheap-review).

### 3. HostGator — best pure entry price
HostGator's budget plans are the cheapest way into hosting in some markets, with unmetered bandwidth and familiar cPanel. Performance is middling (410ms TTFB in our India tests) and renewal jumps are aggressive — fine for a small portfolio, not for anything traffic-heavy. Read the [HostGator review](/reviews/hostgator-review).

### 4. Bluehost — the beginner option
Bluehost is WordPress.org's official pick and starts near $2.95/mo with a free domain and guided onboarding. It's slower than LiteSpeed rivals (680ms TTFB) and upselling is aggressive, but the hand-holding is real. Read the [Bluehost review](/reviews/bluehost-review) or see the [Hostinger vs Bluehost](/comparisons/hostinger-vs-bluehost) data.

### 5. A2 Hosting — best cheap "Turbo" plan
A2's Startup Turbo plan uses LiteSpeed + NVMe for roughly $3/mo intro, with an anytime money-back guarantee. Speeds are close to Hostinger's. Read the [A2 Hosting review](/reviews/a2-hosting-review).

## What you give up on cheap plans

- **Storage**: 10–100GB instead of "unlimited" marketing claims
- **CPU/RAM**: usually 1 core and ~1GB RAM — fine for one site, tight for WooCommerce
- **Support**: still 24/7 chat, but fewer channels than premium tiers
- **Free domain**: often not included on the absolute cheapest tier

The [best cheap hosting](/best/cheap-hosting) rankings and the [₹100 hosting tier](/best/hosting-under-100) show the exact plan-level tradeoffs.

## The renewal trap

Every budget host advertises a discounted intro price tied to a 24–48 month commitment. Hostinger's ₹69/mo plan renews at ₹159/mo; HostGator's ₹99/mo renews at ₹249/mo. Always check the renewal price before committing — our [cheap hosting](/best/cheap-hosting) page exposes these numbers for every plan.

## Cheap vs free WordPress hosting

If you're choosing between cheap and [free WordPress hosting](/tutorials/free-wordpress-hosting), pay the $3. The free tiers come with ads, subdomain-only URLs, no email, and no backups. $3/mo removes every one of those problems.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the cheapest reliable WordPress hosting?**
Hostinger's $2.99/mo plan is the cheapest we'd put a real website on. Below that, you're in free-hosting territory with real reliability risks.

**Is cheap WordPress hosting fast enough?**
Yes — modern LiteSpeed + NVMe budget hosts (Hostinger, A2) post TTFB under 200ms. Older Apache-based plans (Bluehost, HostGator) sit at 400–700ms.

**How much should I expect to pay for WordPress hosting per month?**
$2–6/mo for good shared hosting, $20–35/mo for managed, and $5–20/mo for a VPS. Most sites are perfectly served by the $2–6 range.

**Can I run WooCommerce on cheap WordPress hosting?**
Small stores, yes; busy ones, no. For anything serious, use a VPS or managed plan — see our [WooCommerce hosting](/tutorials/woocommerce-hosting) guide.

**How do I migrate to a cheap host?**
Most budget hosts include a free migration. Our [move WordPress to a new host](/tutorials/move-wordpress-to-new-host) tutorial covers the DIY route.
