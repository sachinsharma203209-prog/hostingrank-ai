## Why refund guarantees matter

A money-back guarantee turns hosting from a gamble into a trial. It's your test-drive window: sign up, run a real site (or our [speed tests](/tutorials/wordpress-hosting-speed-testing)), and leave free if the host disappoints. The differences between hosts are huge:

| Host | Guarantee | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A2 Hosting | **Anytime** | Prorated refund at any point — the strongest in the industry |
| DreamHost | **97 days** | Over 3 months to test |
| HostGator | 45 days | Above-average |
| Hostinger | 30 days | Standard |
| SiteGround | 30 days | Standard |
| Bluehost | 30 days | Standard |
| Kinsta | 30 days | Managed plans |
| Cloudways | 3-day trial | Pay-as-you-go, no lock-in |

Read the guarantees in each [provider review](/reviews) — and remember **the number that matters is what you'd actually experience**, which is what our [benchmarks](/benchmarks) measure.

## What guarantees usually don't cover

Before you rely on one, know the exclusions:

- **Domains** — registration fees are typically non-refundable (see [domains and hosting](/tutorials/wordpress-hosting-and-domain))
- **Add-ons** — SSL upgrades, backups add-ons, email products
- **Usage-based costs** — overages and overlimit charges
- **Setup fees** — rare, but check

A $60 domain + hosting bundle refunding $45 after the domain is registered is a real surprise people hit. Read the terms before checkout.

## How to claim a refund

1. **Check the window** — note your signup date; most hosts use calendar days
2. **Cancel in the panel or support ticket** — don't just stop paying; that can trigger collections
3. **Request the refund explicitly** — some hosts refund automatically, some don't until you ask
4. **Watch the timeline** — refunds typically process in 5–10 business days
5. **Back up first** — your site files may be deleted at cancellation. Take a [backup](/tutorials/wordpress-backups) and move out with the [migration guide](/tutorials/wordpress-hosting-migration)

## The anytime guarantee (A2 Hosting)

A2's **anytime money-back** is unique: cancel mid-term and get a prorated refund of unused time, no 30-day deadline. It's the low-risk option if you want to try a host without committing — that's one reason it leads our [cheap WordPress hosting](/tutorials/cheap-wordpress-hosting) analysis for cautious buyers.

## Use the guarantee as a test window

The best use of a guarantee is a real trial:

1. Sign up on the cheapest plan that fits
2. Install WordPress and a test theme ([install guide](/tutorials/how-to-install-wordpress))
3. Run our [benchmark method](/tutorials/wordpress-hosting-speed-testing) — TTFB from your region, uptime for a couple of weeks
4. If it clears the bar, keep it. If not, refund and [migrate](/tutorials/wordpress-hosting-migration) to a host that does

## Frequently asked questions

**How long is the Hostinger refund period?**
30 days from purchase. Domains and some add-ons are excluded. Details in the [Hostinger review](/reviews/hostinger-review).

**Which hosting has the longest money-back guarantee?**
DreamHost's 97 days, followed by A2's anytime guarantee (prorated at any point) and HostGator's 45 days. Compare all hosts in [best WordPress hosting](/best/wordpress-hosting).

**Does a money-back guarantee include the domain?**
No — domain registration fees are almost always non-refundable. Keep your domain at a separate registrar ([domains and hosting](/tutorials/wordpress-hosting-and-domain)) so a refund doesn't cost you the domain.

**Can I get a refund after the guarantee period?**
Mostly no, except A2's anytime guarantee (prorated). After the window, the host keeps the fees — which is why the guarantee length matters at signup.

**What should I do before canceling hosting for a refund?**
Take a full [backup](/tutorials/wordpress-backups), move any email elsewhere (see [email setup](/tutorials/wordpress-email-setup)), and confirm the refund terms in writing.
