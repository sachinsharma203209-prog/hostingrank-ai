## WordPress hosting plans, decoded

Every WordPress hosting plan is a combination of four things: **server type, resources, management, and price**. Once you understand those four dials, any provider's pricing page becomes readable.

- **Server type** — shared, VPS, dedicated, or cloud
- **Resources** — CPU, RAM, storage type, bandwidth, site limits
- **Management** — self-managed, partially managed, fully managed
- **Price** — intro vs renewal, term length, and add-ons

## The four main WordPress hosting plan types

### 1. Shared WordPress hosting ($2–6/mo)
Your site shares a server with others. Cheapest, easiest, and fine for most blogs and small business sites. Modern shared plans (LiteSpeed + NVMe) are fast; old Apache-based ones are slow. Start with our [best shared hosting](/best/shared-hosting) guide.

### 2. Managed WordPress hosting ($20–35/mo)
Automatic updates, backups, staging, security, and expert support. Kinsta, WP Engine, and Cloudways are the names. Full comparison in our [managed WordPress hosting](/tutorials/managed-wordpress-hosting) guide.

### 3. VPS WordPress hosting ($5–20/mo)
Dedicated CPU and RAM with root access. For sites that outgrow shared resources. See [best VPS hosting](/best/vps-hosting).

### 4. Cloud WordPress hosting ($12–35/mo)
Scalable resources across many servers — the middle ground for growing stores and apps. See [best cloud hosting](/best/cloud-hosting).

## What's actually in a WordPress hosting plan

A typical budget shared plan includes:

- **Storage**: 10–100GB NVMe/SSD
- **Bandwidth**: unmetered or 100GB–1TB
- **Websites**: 1 to unlimited
- **SSL**: free (Let's Encrypt)
- **Email accounts**: 0 to unlimited
- **Backups**: weekly or daily
- **Domain**: often free for year one

Managed plans add: staging, automatic updates, CDN, malware scanning, and priority support.

## How to read a plan's fine print

- **Intro vs renewal price** — the price doubles or triples after year one. Always check renewal.
- **Term commitment** — ₹69/mo might mean 48 months upfront. Shorter terms cost more per month.
- **Site limits** — "unlimited websites" usually still caps CPU/RAM.
- **Storage type** — NVMe > SSD > HDD, in that order.
- **Free domain** — not on the cheapest tier of many hosts.

## Which plan should you buy?

- **1 site, low traffic** → entry shared plan (Hostinger Single)
- **Blog or small business** → shared Premium tier with free domain
- **WooCommerce** → Business shared or a small VPS
- **High traffic / revenue-critical** → managed (Kinsta, WP Engine) or Cloudways
- **Developer** → VPS or Cloudways

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the difference between cheap and expensive WordPress hosting plans?**
You're paying for resources, management, and support. Cheap plans share a server and you handle updates and backups yourself; premium plans isolate resources and automate everything.

**Do WordPress hosting plans include a free domain?**
Often on mid-tier plans (Hostinger Premium, Bluehost Basic), rarely on the cheapest tier.

**Can I upgrade my WordPress hosting plan later?**
Yes — most hosts let you upgrade in place. Cloudways and VPS hosts can scale resources instantly; shared hosts typically require moving to a higher plan.
